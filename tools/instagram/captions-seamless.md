# Evolution24 Properties on Instagram: the seamless grid

Nine posts that join up into one picture on the profile, drawn from the website. Across the
top row is the site's hero: the logo, "An evolution in *home*." and the numbers. Below it,
the building mark stands as a skyline the width of the grid, and each of its four buildings is
filled with a photo of one of ours: 181 St. Paul, The Biltmore, 121 Park Drive and Charlotte
Square. They stand on a tan line over a strip of ink that carries the phone number, the website
and "Find your *place*."

The pictures run across the edges between posts; the words never do, so each post still reads
on its own in the feed. Everything here comes from evolution24.net, with no prices or
availability counts, because those go out of date.

## How to post the grid

Instagram puts the newest post at the top left, so you build the grid from the bottom right up.

1. **Post them in file order, 01 first and 09 last.** Post each one as a single photo, not a
   carousel, and post all nine in one sitting so nothing lands in between.
2. **Keep the whole picture.** The posts are 3:4 (1080 × 1440), the shape of the profile grid.
   Before you tap Next, check that the preview shows the whole post. If it shows a square, tap
   the crop button at the bottom left of the preview.
3. **Add the details for each post:** the caption, the location, and the alt text. Alt text is
   under Advanced settings → Accessibility → Write alt text, and each one below fits Instagram's
   100-character limit.
4. **Check the grid after every third post.** Each row should line up into one picture. If a post
   lands in the wrong spot, tap and hold it, choose Reorder grid, and drag it into place.
5. **Unpin anything pinned, and don't pin these.** Pinned posts sit at the top of the grid and
   would push the picture out of line.
6. **Plan for later posts.** Each new post shifts the picture along the grid. Post in threes to
   keep it whole, or use Reorder grid to put it back together.
7. **Post from the Instagram app.** Some scheduling tools crop 3:4 images to 4:5.

`grid-gaps.jpg` shows the nine as the profile will, and `profile-preview.png` shows them on a
phone in light and dark mode. Instagram now counts only five hashtags per post, so each caption
has five or fewer.
